- Sequence Model Imitation Learning with Unobserved Contexts — Swamy et al., NeurIPS 2022 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02225 | Abstract and [§§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.02225) read. The paper formalizes hidden-context imitation over histories, analyzes incorrect context posteriors, and develops on-policy moment-matching approaches. | Section 3 names the latching effect: off-policy learners interpret their own mistaken past actions as expert evidence. Observation 3 connects more expert mistakes to improved trajectory coverage. This occupies the mechanism linking demonstration histories, execution deviations and failed recovery; it does not construct I9's observation-matched recovery pairs.
- IntervenGen: Interventional Data Generation for Robust and Data-Efficient Robot Imitation Learning — Hoque et al., IROS 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01472 | Abstract and [§§III–VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.01472) read. The generator executes a BC-RNN to produce mistakes, transforms human recovery segments into the new object frame, and retrains on generated interventions. | Section VI explicitly explains that accurate human demonstrations omit recovery under robot observation errors. The nut-geometry task has identical observed poses with different hidden geometries; failed-grasp feedback enables recovery. This is a close occupant of both the data-generation payoff and partial-observability setting. It selects recoveries by intervention generation, rather than an explicit observation-matched history-identifiability criterion.
- CCDP: Composition of Conditional Diffusion Policies with Guided Sampling — Razmjoo et al., IROS 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15386 | Abstract and [§§IV-A–IV-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15386) read. Successful demonstrations train component action distributions; a synthetic auxiliary dataset trains recovery, and known failure features condition compositional sampling. | The introduction already identifies the missing-failure-history problem. Failure-dependent guidance avoids repeating previously unsuccessful actions, including unknown-direction door opening and button search. Its recovery construction assumes static failure causes and supplied failure-feature extraction; it does not collect I9's paired histories. Its results also show that successful-only source demonstrations can support recovery when supplemented by this structure.
- Deconfounding Imitation Learning with Variational Inference — Vuorio et al., revised 2024 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02667 | Abstract and [§§3.3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02667) read. A latent dynamics/inference model is learned from exploration; a latent-conditioned policy is learned from expert demonstrations. | Sections 3.3–3.5 formalize the erroneous treatment of self-generated actions as latent-state evidence. Section 4.1 requires recurrence for identification from demonstrations alone; §4.2 uses exploration to address missing state/action coverage. This directly precedes a history-identifiability argument, but uses a learned latent model rather than matched-history selection. The original preprint title was “Deconfounded Imitation Learning.”
- Fighting Copycat Agents in Behavioral Cloning from Observation Histories — Wen et al., 2020 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14876 | Abstract and [§§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.14876) read. A target-conditioned adversary suppresses excess information about the previous expert action in the history representation. | Section 4 explains how temporally correlated expert actions create a shortcut that improves held-out demonstration likelihood while degrading deployed behavior. It supplies an existing causal account of history-training failure, but its intervention is representation regularization and its claim is not that all missing recovery behavior is caused by insufficient memory.
- DART: Noise Injection for Robust Imitation Learning — Laskey et al., CoRL 2017 | https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09327 | Abstract and [§4, including the shift bound and noise-distribution objective](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/laskey17a/laskey17a.pdf), read. Noise is injected into supervisor controls so corrective behavior enters the training distribution. | Recovery-data generation from otherwise successful supervision is established in manipulation and locomotion. DART adjusts perturbations to approximate learner error, rather than selecting histories with identical current observations and conflicting recovery labels.
- Learning Long-Context Diffusion Policies via Past-Token Prediction — Torne et al., 2025 | https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09561 | Abstract and [§§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09561) read. The policy jointly predicts past and future action tokens; visual embeddings are cached for policy-head training, and past-action consistency can rank inference samples. | Section 3 finds an opposing history failure: diffusion policies underuse action dependencies where regression policies exhibit copycat behavior. Thus failure with longer histories already has a published explanation that does not depend on omitted recovery demonstrations. This is a relevant long-context training baseline, without an observation-matched recovery dataset.
- DART author technical blog — non-paper artifact | https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2017/10/26/dart/ | The explanations of compounding errors, noisy demonstration collection, grasping and bed-making applications were read. | The blog explicitly attributes driving recovery failure to demonstrations that stayed in the lane center and omitted roadside states.
